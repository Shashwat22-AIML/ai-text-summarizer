SAMPLE 3 — What is a Black Hole?

• A black hole is like a super-strong vacuum cleaner in space — its gravity is so powerful that nothing, not even light, can escape once it gets too close.

• The "point of no return" is called the event horizon. Cross it, and you're gone!

• Black holes are born when a really big star (way bigger than our Sun) runs out of fuel and collapses. Then it goes BOOM (a supernova), and if what's left is heavy enough, it squishes into a black hole.

• Some black holes are small-ish (just a few times heavier than the Sun). Others — called supermassive black holes — sit in the middle of galaxies and are billions of times heavier. Scientists aren't totally sure how those giants formed.

• We can't see black holes directly because they don't glow. But we can tell they're there by watching how they pull on nearby stars and gas. In 2019, we took the very first photo of a black hole's "shadow" — a huge deal!
